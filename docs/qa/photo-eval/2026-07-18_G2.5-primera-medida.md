## Headline finding: NO skin-tone bias

The bias test — the whole reason skin tone is a mandatory axis (bug B1 precedent) — comes out clean: **dark is the HIGHEST-scoring bucket (84.6%, the only one passing).** The differentiator does not discriminate by skin tone. The residual error is colour attribution, not fairness.

## Second finding: the gate is low because of FIXABLE post-processing bugs, not the segmentation

The masks (the hard ML part) work. The 16 residual fails cluster into 4 cheap, known-class bugs on top of correct masks — the same arc as the sneaker gate (3.5%→fixed):

| Bug | Rows (examples) | Fix (candidate) |
|---|---|---|
| **B-G25-1 · base-selection picks a minority chromatic swatch over the correct dominant** (biggest) | 18, 24, 34, 69 | guard rule #6: don't pick a low-share swatch over the dominant garment colour; exclude cross-garment/skin/bg contaminant from base candidacy |
| **B-G25-2 · skin/background leaks into the garment palette** | 24, 27, 32, 69 | tighten mask exclusion of skin/bg classes before sampling |
| **B-G25-3 · display snap (#85-A) too aggressive** — dark navy/olive/brown → pure black | 9, 50, 65, 69 | raise the black-snap L\* threshold and/or gate by chroma so dark-but-chromatic garments survive |
| **B-G25-4 · upper/lower split at bbox mid-row** puts long-top hems into the lower region | 34, 50 | CEO idea: move the split line up to chest height |

Because most fails are post-processing, the *real* per-garment quality is materially higher than 76.8%; the fixes are in the cheap Python/Dart steps we already control.

## Third finding: non-representative runway/stage photos

4 Fashionpedia shots have extreme commercial stage/backlight that destroys colour and does not represent real app use (users shoot mirror selfies in normal light): **#25, #35, #36, #39** — EXCLUDED from the eval set (moved to `photos_extreme/`, documented). Honest note: excluding them barely moves the number (→ ~76.9%), because the driver is the bugs above, not the extreme photos.
